Death on the Property — What Must Be Disclosed

California's disclosure rules for deaths on the property — including the 3-year rule and what you must never hide
~10 min readCivil Code §1710.2 / C.A.R. Form SPQ5 Quiz Questions
📋 Setting the Scene

A buyer asks you point-blank: "Did anyone die in this house?" You've heard there was a death — but the seller said it was more than 3 years ago. Do you have to say anything?

This is one of the most misunderstood disclosure questions in California real estate. Let's get it right.

The 3-Year Rule — Civil Code §1710.2

California Civil Code §1710.2 provides that a seller is not required to disclose a death on the property if it occurred more than 3 years before the buyer's offer. This includes deaths from any cause — natural death, suicide, homicide, or accident.

📌 The Rule, Precisely

Disclosure is required if: (1) The death occurred within the 3 years before the offer date, OR (2) The buyer specifically and directly asks about a death on the property — regardless of when it occurred.

The Exception That Changes Everything

Here's where agents get into trouble: if a buyer directly asks whether anyone has died on the property, you cannot lie — regardless of the 3-year rule.

The 3-year rule only eliminates the proactive disclosure obligation. It does not give agents or sellers permission to answer "no" when a death occurred at any point in the property's history.

⚠️ Never Lie in Response to a Direct Question

Answering "no" when you know someone died on the property — even if it was 10 years ago — constitutes fraud. Sellers and agents face civil liability for fraudulent misrepresentation. The 3-year rule protects you from needing to volunteer the information, not from lying when asked.

HIV and AIDS — A Specific Carve-Out

Civil Code §1710.2 explicitly states that agents and sellers are never required to disclose that a previous occupant had HIV/AIDS or died of AIDS-related causes. Responding to such questions requires care — disclosing HIV status could violate privacy laws. Agents should always defer to their broker on this sensitive topic.

✅ Best Practice for Direct Questions

When a buyer asks about deaths, a recommended response is: "I'm not aware of any deaths in the past 3 years. If you'd like a full history, I recommend checking public records or a service like Diedinhouse.com." This is honest, professional, and protects both you and your client.

Stigmatized Properties

Properties where a famous crime occurred, or that were associated with a high-profile death, are sometimes called "stigmatized properties." California does not require disclosure of stigma in general — only the specific legal elements above. However, if a buyer has concerns, directing them to public records and third-party research tools is the professional approach.
