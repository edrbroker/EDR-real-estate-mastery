Mello-Roos, HOA, and Special Tax Disclosures

Hidden costs that can surprise buyers — and your duty to make sure they're not hidden
~10 min readGovernment Code §53340.2 / Civil Code §1368 / RPA ¶N(3)5 Quiz Questions
📋 Setting the Scene

Marcus and Priya Okonkwo are budgeting carefully for 5555 Winners Lane. They've accounted for their mortgage, insurance, and property taxes. Then their agent drops a bombshell: "Oh, there's also a Mello-Roos special tax of $3,400 per year."

Priya's jaw drops. "Why didn't anyone tell us this sooner?"

Because their agent didn't review the disclosures carefully enough. Don't be that agent.

What Is Mello-Roos?

Mello-Roos is a special tax levied by a Community Facilities District (CFD) under the Mello-Roos Community Facilities Act of 1982. Cities and counties use Mello-Roos to fund infrastructure and services in new developments — things like roads, schools, parks, and emergency services.

📌 Key Facts About Mello-Roos

Mello-Roos is separate from regular property taxes. It shows up as a line item on the property tax bill. It can be substantial — $1,000 to $6,000+ per year in some California communities. It does NOT disappear when you pay off your mortgage.

Under Government Code §53340.2, sellers are required to disclose Mello-Roos assessments. This is typically included in the NHD report, but agents should verify it independently by reviewing the property tax bill and/or the preliminary title report.

HOA Disclosures

If a property belongs to a Homeowner's Association, California Civil Code §1368 requires the seller to provide the buyer with a comprehensive package of HOA documents, including:

  • CC&Rs (Covenants, Conditions & Restrictions)
  • HOA bylaws and articles of incorporation
  • Current HOA budget and reserve fund study
  • Recent meeting minutes (past 12 months)
  • Any pending special assessments
  • Current monthly dues and any planned increases
  • Pending litigation involving the HOA
RPA Reference: RPA ¶N(3) requires the seller to order HOA documents within 3 calendar days of acceptance. The seller pays the HOA document fee. Buyer has a right to cancel within the HOA review period after receiving the complete package.
⚠️ Reserve Fund Alert

A low reserve fund is a red flag. If the HOA reserve fund is underfunded (below 30% of the required threshold is common), the association may levy a special assessment — meaning every homeowner, including your client, could face a one-time charge of thousands of dollars. Always review the reserve study.

✅ Agent Best Practice

Pull the property tax bill early. Show your buyer every line — regular property tax, Mello-Roos, any 1915 Act bonds, and special assessment districts. Then estimate the total monthly carry with ALL costs included. Buyers who are surprised by these costs after closing blame their agent — and sometimes sue.
