Trust Accounts and Agent Responsibilities

How California law governs earnest money, broker trust accounts, and what agents can and cannot do
~10 min readBusiness & Professions Code §10145 / DRE Regs5 Quiz Questions
📋 The EMD Question

Your buyer Marcus hands you a personal check for the $37,500 earnest money deposit. He says, "Can you just hold onto this for us until we know the deal is solid?" You know the answer — and it's not what Marcus is expecting.

What Is a Trust Account?

Under California law (Business & Professions Code §10145), real estate brokers who handle client funds must maintain a separate trust account — completely separate from the broker's operating account. Money in a trust account belongs to the clients, not the broker. Commingling (mixing client funds with business funds) is illegal and grounds for license revocation.

The EMD Process

The Earnest Money Deposit must be handled very specifically under the RPA:

  • Delivered to escrow (or the listing broker's trust account) within the time specified in the contract (typically 3 business days of acceptance)
  • The check is typically made out to the escrow company or the listing broker's trust account — NOT to the seller personally
  • Once in escrow, the funds cannot be released without written instructions from both parties (or a court order)
⚠️ Never Hold Funds Personally

As a salesperson, you cannot hold client funds. You are not licensed to maintain a trust account. Only your broker can do that. If Marcus hands you a check, it goes immediately to your broker or directly to escrow — never into your personal account.

DRE Trust Account Rules

The DRE takes trust account violations extremely seriously. Key rules include:

  • Funds must be deposited into the trust account within 3 business days of receipt
  • Brokers must reconcile trust accounts monthly
  • No funds can be disbursed without written authorization from the party who deposited the money
  • Brokers cannot pay personal expenses from a trust account
DRE Reference: Business & Professions Code §10145 and Commissioner's Regulation §2950-2951 govern trust account requirements. The DRE found trust fund recordkeeping violations in 57% of audits in fiscal year 2023-24 — one of the most common violation areas.
