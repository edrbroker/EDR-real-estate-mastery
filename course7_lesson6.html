The Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure

Two federal documents every buyer receives — and what agents need to know about both
~10 min readTRID / RESPA / Reg Z (TILA) / HUD5 Quiz Questions
📋 Setting the Scene

Three days after Marcus and Priya submit their loan application, their lender sends them a 3-page document called the Loan Estimate. Marcus emails it to you: "Is this normal? What does it all mean?"

It's completely normal — it's federally required. And understanding it is how you help your clients catch errors before they become problems.

What Is TRID?

TRID stands for TILA-RESPA Integrated Disclosure — a federal rule that combined the Truth-in-Lending Act (TILA) disclosures and the Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act (RESPA) disclosures into two unified documents:

  1. Loan Estimate (LE) — given within 3 business days of loan application
  2. Closing Disclosure (CD) — given at least 3 business days before closing

These rules exist to protect borrowers from surprise costs and to allow comparison shopping between lenders.

The Loan Estimate — Within 3 Business Days of Application

The Loan Estimate gives your buyers an estimate of what their loan will cost. Key sections:

LE SectionWhat It Shows
Page 1 — Loan TermsLoan amount, interest rate, monthly payment, whether rate/payment can increase
Page 2 — Projected PaymentsEstimated monthly payment breakdown: principal + interest + PMI + escrow
Page 2 — Closing CostsItemized list of origination charges, third-party fees, prepaid items
Page 3 — ComparisonsAPR, total interest paid over life of loan, total loan costs comparison
📌 APR vs. Interest Rate

The Annual Percentage Rate (APR) is always higher than the interest rate because it includes fees. APR is the true "cost" of the loan expressed as a percentage. When comparing two loan options, compare APRs — not just interest rates — for an apples-to-apples comparison.

The Closing Disclosure — At Least 3 Business Days Before Closing

The Closing Disclosure is the final version of all loan costs — it must be delivered at least 3 business days before the loan consummation date. If material changes occur after delivery (change in loan product, rate, or new prepayment penalty added), the 3-day clock resets.

Key items to review on the CD:

  • Final loan amount — does it match what was approved?
  • Interest rate — does it match what was locked?
  • Cash to Close — how much does the buyer need to wire?
  • Seller credits — is the agreed credit correctly applied?
  • Commission amounts — are they correct?
  • Prepaid property taxes and insurance — are they correctly calculated?
⚠️ A Wrong CD Can Delay Closing

Errors on the Closing Disclosure can push close of escrow by days. As the buyer's agent, request the CD as soon as it's available (typically 48–72 hours before the scheduled close) and review it BEFORE your clients see it. Catching an error early means you have time to fix it without blowing the close date.

✅ The Tolerance Rules

Under TRID, certain closing cost categories have "tolerance" limits — meaning the final CD cannot exceed the Loan Estimate by more than a set amount. If lender fees increase beyond the zero-tolerance limit, the lender must cure the difference. Know that these rules exist, and refer your clients to their lender or a HUD-approved housing counselor if they have concerns.
