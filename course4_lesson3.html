How Escrow Closes

The step-by-step sequence from signing loan docs to recording the deed — what your client needs to know
~10 min readRPA ¶3B / ¶14G / ¶165 Quiz Questions
📋 The Home Stretch

It's Day 28. Marcus and Priya have removed all contingencies on 5555 Winners Lane. Their loan is approved, the appraisal came in at value, and the inspection issues were resolved. Now the escrow officer calls: "Loan docs are in. We're scheduling the signing." This is the moment your clients have been waiting for. You need to walk them through exactly what happens next.

The Closing Sequence

Closing escrow is a multi-step process that happens in a specific order. One step can't happen until the one before it is complete.

StepWhat HappensWho Does It
1. Loan Docs SentLender sends final loan documents to escrowBuyer's lender
2. Loan Doc SigningBuyer signs all loan documents (usually at escrow office or with a notary)Buyer
3. Loan FundingLender wires the loan proceeds to escrow (1-2 days after signing)Lender
4. Buyer's Closing FundsBuyer wires their down payment and closing costs to escrowBuyer
5. Final WalkthroughBuyer does final verification of property condition (RPA ¶16)Buyer + Agent
6. RecordingEscrow authorizes county recorder to record the grant deedEscrow / Title
7. DisbursementEscrow releases seller proceeds, pays commissions, pays off liensEscrow
8. KeysSeller vacates, buyer gets keys (per possession terms in RPA)Both agents

The Final Walkthrough

Under RPA ¶16, the buyer has the right to do a final verification of condition within 5 days before close of escrow. This is not a contingency — it's a verification that the property is in the same condition as when the offer was made, repairs have been completed, and the seller has complied with their obligations.

⚠️ Common Mistake

New agents sometimes skip the final walkthrough because the deal is almost done and they don't want to rock the boat. Don't skip it. If the seller took the built-in refrigerator that was supposed to stay, or left a pile of junk in the garage, you want to catch it before keys are handed over — not after.

Recording = Ownership

The most important moment in the entire transaction is recording. When the county recorder stamps the grant deed with the recording time, the buyer legally owns the property. Until that happens, nothing is final — no matter what anyone tells you.

📌 Key Rule

In California, possession typically transfers at close of escrow (recording), unless otherwise negotiated. The seller should be fully moved out by the time recording happens unless the RPA specifies a different possession date.

What Can Delay Closing?

  • Lender hasn't funded yet (loan docs signed but wire not sent)
  • Buyer's wire hasn't arrived at escrow in time for same-day recording
  • Last-minute title issues (new lien discovered)
  • Seller hasn't signed their documents
  • County recorder's office closes early or is backed up
✓ Best Practice

Tell your buyers to wire their closing funds at least 24–48 hours before the scheduled close date. Banks have daily wire cutoff times (usually 2–3 PM), and wires sent after cutoff won't arrive until the next business day — which can push closing by a full day.
