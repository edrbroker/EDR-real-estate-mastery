Agency Disclosure and Relationships

Who does each agent represent? California's agency disclosure requirements every licensee must master
~10 min readCivil Code §2079.13–§2079.24 / C.A.R. Form AD5 Quiz Questions
📋 Setting the Scene

The Okonkwos are writing an offer on 5555 Winners Lane. Their agent hands them a form called the Disclosure Regarding Real Estate Agency Relationship. Marcus asks: "Who exactly are you working for?"

It's the most important question a buyer can ask — and California law requires a formal written answer.

California Agency Law

Under California Civil Code §2079.13–§2079.24, every real estate transaction must include a clear written disclosure of who each agent represents. The three possible agency relationships in California are:

Agency TypeWho the Agent RepresentsDuties Owed
Seller's Agent (Listing Agent)The seller exclusivelyFiduciary duty to seller; limited duty to buyer (honesty, fair dealing)
Buyer's AgentThe buyer exclusivelyFiduciary duty to buyer; limited duty to seller (honesty, fair dealing)
Dual AgentBoth buyer and sellerFiduciary duty to both — but conflicts must be managed; must not disclose price limits without consent
📌 Fiduciary Duty Defined

A fiduciary owes their client loyalty, confidentiality, disclosure, obedience, reasonable care, and accounting. As the buyer's agent, your fiduciary duty runs to the Okonkwos — not the seller, not the listing broker, not even Rashaad Dixon's brokerage if you have a dual agency situation.

The Agency Disclosure Form (C.A.R. Form AD)

California requires agents to provide the Disclosure Regarding Real Estate Agency Relationships (C.A.R. Form AD) at the earliest of:

  • For listing agents: when the listing agreement is executed
  • For buyer's agents: before entering into a representation agreement
  • In all cases: before or when the purchase offer is prepared
⚠️ Timing Matters

Delivering the agency disclosure at the signing table is too late. California courts have held that late disclosure can affect the enforceability of the agency relationship and expose agents to liability. Deliver it early and get a signature.

Dual Agency — Managing the Conflict

Dual agency occurs when the same agent or same brokerage represents both buyer and seller in the same transaction. In California, dual agency is legal with informed written consent from both parties — but it creates inherent conflicts.

Under Civil Code §2079.21, a dual agent cannot, without consent:

  • Disclose that the seller will accept less than the listing price
  • Disclose that the buyer will pay more than the offered price
  • Disclose confidential information that either party has not consented to share
✅ Practical Guidance

If you find yourself in a dual agency situation, loop in your broker immediately. EDR Realty Group must provide written dual agency consent before proceeding. Never try to manage this alone as a new agent.
