Structuring a Competitive Offer

Beyond price — the terms and conditions that make or break an offer in California
~10 min readRPA ¶3A-3F / ¶5A-5C / ¶145 Quiz Questions
📋 Setting the Scene

The Okonkwos are ready to write. They've seen the comps — 5555 Winners Lane is worth the $1,250,000 ask in this market. But another agent from a competing brokerage is also writing an offer today.

Price matters. But the offer that wins is often the one with the cleanest, most attractive terms — not just the highest number.

The Key Elements of a Strong Offer

Every offer in California is governed by the RPA. Here's how each key term affects competitiveness:

RPA TermParagraphCompetitive Strategy
Purchase Price¶3APrice at or above list in seller's markets; supported by comps
Close of Escrow¶3BMatch seller's timeline — ask the listing agent what they prefer
Earnest Money Deposit (EMD)¶3D(1) / ¶5AHigher EMD signals commitment; 2–3% is competitive; 1% is minimum
Down Payment¶3FMore down = stronger buyer; all-cash is strongest
Loan Type¶3EConventional is stronger than FHA/VA in competitive markets
Contingency Periods¶14Shorter periods = stronger offer; 17-day default is standard
Offer Expiration¶3C / ¶32A3-day default; short expiration creates urgency

The Earnest Money Deposit — Your Signal of Seriousness

The EMD is more than just a deposit — it's your first signal to the seller about how serious your client is. Under RPA ¶3D(1), the EMD must be delivered within 3 business days of acceptance by wire transfer.

📌 EMD Math for 5555 Winners Lane

At $1,250,000: A 1% EMD = $12,500 (minimum, looks thin). A 2% EMD = $25,000 (competitive). A 3% EMD = $37,500 (strong signal). Maximum liquidated damages = 3% of purchase price under RPA ¶29.

Loan Type Matters

In a multiple offer situation, loan type affects seller perception of risk:

  • All cash: No appraisal contingency required; fastest close; strongest offer
  • Conventional 20%+ down: Strong; appraised value risk lower for seller
  • Conventional less than 20%: Acceptable; PMI required but doesn't affect seller
  • FHA/VA: More seller concession restrictions; appraisal required; some sellers reluctant
✅ Framing Your Offer to Win

When you submit an offer, attach a brief "offer summary" — a one-page overview of the key terms that makes it easy for the listing agent to present to the sellers. Include your client's pre-approval letter. A clean, organized offer presentation signals professionalism and seriousness.

RPA Reference: RPA ¶3 governs all terms of purchase and allocation of costs. Review each line with your client before signing — every blank matters.
