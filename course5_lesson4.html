Agent Visual Inspection — Your Legal Duty

California's agent inspection requirement under Civil Code §2079 — and why shortcuts can end your career
~10 min readCivil Code §2079 / C.A.R. Form AVID5 Quiz Questions
📋 Setting the Scene

You're the buyer's agent representing the Okonkwos at 5555 Winners Lane. You've been to the property twice — once for the initial showing and once during the inspection period. The seller's TDS looks clean. Are you done?

Not quite. California law requires you to personally inspect the property and disclose what you observe. This is your independent duty — separate from the seller's.

The Agent Visual Inspection Disclosure (AVID)

California Civil Code §2079 requires both the listing agent and the buyer's agent to conduct a competent and diligent visual inspection of the property and disclose any facts that materially affect value or desirability that a visual inspection would reveal.

The C.A.R. Agent Visual Inspection Disclosure (AVID) form is the standard way agents document this inspection.

📌 Key Phrase: "Competent and Diligent"

The law doesn't require you to be a home inspector or structural engineer. But you must walk the property with open eyes and document what you see. Rushing through, delegating to an assistant, or never visiting the property at all creates serious liability.

What Must You Inspect?

Your visual inspection should cover all accessible areas of the property, including:

  • Exterior: foundation cracks, drainage issues, roof condition (from ground level), grading
  • Interior: water stains, visible mold, cracks in walls or ceilings, uneven floors
  • Garage: unpermitted additions, fire separation issues
  • Yard: drainage, retaining walls, pools/spas condition
  • Neighborhood: traffic, noise, proximity to commercial activity

The 2-Year Statute of Limitations

Under Civil Code §2079, a buyer has 2 years from the date of recordation (close of escrow) to bring a civil action against an agent for failure to conduct or disclose their visual inspection. This means a transaction that closed smoothly can still result in a lawsuit 18 months later.

⚠️ Real Consequence

An agent who skips the visual inspection, lets an assistant fill out the AVID without visiting the property, or glosses over obvious defects can face civil liability, E&O insurance claims, and a DRE complaint. Always do your own inspection. Always document it. Always date it and sign it.

✅ Best Practice

Complete the AVID during your final walkthrough or inspection visit. Take photos. Write specific notes — not just "no visible defects" but "inspected on [date] — no visible cracks, stains, or defects observed in accessible areas." Specificity protects you.
