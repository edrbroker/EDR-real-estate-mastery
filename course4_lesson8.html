When Deals Fall Apart — Cancellation and Deposit Disputes

What happens to the earnest money when a transaction cancels, and how to protect your client
~10 min readRPA ¶14H / ¶29 / Civil Code §1057.35 Quiz Questions
📋 The Call Nobody Wants

It's Day 20. Marcus calls you. Their loan got denied — the lender discovered a tax lien from two years ago that didn't show up in the initial pre-approval. The loan contingency is still active. They want to cancel. Now you have to navigate getting their $37,500 EMD back.

Cancellation With Active Contingencies

If a buyer cancels while a contingency is still active and has not been removed in writing, they are generally entitled to a full return of their EMD. This is one of the most important protections in the RPA — contingencies exist to protect the buyer's deposit.

📌 Key Rule

In Marcus and Priya's case, the loan contingency is still active on Day 20 (default deadline is Day 21). They can cancel in good faith due to the loan denial and are entitled to their full EMD back.

The Cancellation Process

Cancellation requires both parties to sign a Cancellation of Contract (CC form) and submit mutual cancellation instructions to escrow. This is important: escrow needs instructions from BOTH sides to release the deposit.

  • Buyer sends a written notice of cancellation
  • Both buyer and seller sign the CC form
  • Both sign mutual cancellation escrow instructions
  • Escrow releases the deposit per those instructions
⚠️ When the Seller Refuses to Sign

If the seller refuses to sign the cancellation instructions without good cause, they may be liable for a $1,000 civil penalty under Civil Code §1057.3. However, this is a legal matter — tell your client to consult an attorney if the seller is unreasonably withholding their signature.

Liquidated Damages — When the Buyer Breaches

If all contingencies have been removed and the buyer breaches the contract (refuses to close for no valid reason), the seller may be entitled to keep the EMD as liquidated damages under RPA ¶29.

  • The liquidated damages clause must be initialed by both parties to be enforceable
  • The amount is capped at 3% of the purchase price for 1-to-4 unit residential properties
  • On a $1,250,000 purchase: 3% = $37,500 (the entire standard EMD)
RPA ¶29: Liquidated damages apply only if: (1) the clause is initialed, (2) all contingencies have been removed, and (3) the buyer breaches. If any contingency was still active, the buyer can cancel without penalty.
