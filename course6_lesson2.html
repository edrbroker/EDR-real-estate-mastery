Reading the Market — Pricing Strategy

How to price an offer correctly using comps, market conditions, and data — not emotion
~10 min readRPA ¶3A / DRE Market Analysis Standards5 Quiz Questions
📋 Setting the Scene

The Okonkwos have toured 5555 Winners Lane twice. They love it. Marcus says, "What's it worth? Should we offer the full list price or go lower?"

This is the question every buyer asks — and every agent needs to answer with data, not feelings.

What Is a Comparative Market Analysis (CMA)?

A Comparative Market Analysis (CMA) is your primary tool for determining what a property is worth in the current market. It compares the subject property to recently sold comparable properties — "comps" — to arrive at a supportable price range.

📌 CMA vs. Appraisal

A CMA is an agent's opinion of value — not a licensed appraisal. It's the tool you use to advise your clients on offer price. The lender's appraiser will do their own formal appraisal after offer acceptance. If the appraisal comes in below the purchase price, that triggers the appraisal contingency.

What Makes a Good Comp?

  • Location: Same neighborhood, ideally same street or within a half mile
  • Size: Within 10–15% of the subject's square footage
  • Condition: Updated vs. dated matters — adjust accordingly
  • Sold date: Ideally within 90 days; 6 months max in a stable market
  • Style: Single-family to single-family; don't mix condo comps with SFR

Reading Market Conditions

Market ConditionSignalOffer Strategy
Seller's MarketLow inventory, multiple offers, homes selling above list price, days on market under 14Price at or above list; minimize contingencies; short close
Balanced MarketModerate inventory, some competition, homes selling near list, 21–45 days on marketPrice at or slightly below list; standard contingencies
Buyer's MarketHigh inventory, properties sitting, price reductions common, 60+ days on marketOffer below list; request credits; strong contingencies
Key Metric: Days on Market (DOM) is your fastest read on market temperature. A new listing in a hot neighborhood has leverage; a listing at 60+ days is a motivated seller waiting for a reasonable offer.

The Price Per Square Foot Formula

A useful starting point (not the final word) is price per square foot. If three comparable homes sold for an average of $650/sq ft and 5555 Winners Lane is 1,800 sq ft, your baseline is $1,170,000. From there you adjust up (updated kitchen, premium lot) or down (deferred maintenance, busy street, older systems).

⚠️ Don't Price on Emotion

Buyers who "fall in love" with a property tend to overpay. Your job is to give them objective data and let them decide. Present the comps, present the range, present your recommendation — and then respect their decision. Over-attachment to one property is the buyer's single biggest negotiating weakness.

✅ Presenting the CMA

Show your clients three numbers: (1) What comparable homes have sold for, (2) Your recommended offer range, (3) What you believe the property will appraise at. This gives them full context to decide their offer price with confidence.
