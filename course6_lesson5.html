Negotiating Inspection Results

How to turn inspection findings into reasonable repair requests — without blowing up the deal
~10 min readRPA ¶14C / C.A.R. Form RR / ¶8B5 Quiz Questions
📋 Setting the Scene

The Okonkwos' inspection report on 5555 Winners Lane comes back — 47 items. Three of them are significant: an aging water heater, a minor foundation crack, and outdated electrical panel. Marcus wants to ask for everything to be fixed. Priya just wants to close.

Your job: guide them to a negotiated outcome that is reasonable, protective, and doesn't kill the deal.

What the Inspection Period Is For

Under RPA ¶14C, the inspection contingency gives buyers the right to investigate the property and either approve it, request repairs, or cancel. The standard period is 17 days after acceptance.

📌 Inspection Period = Due Diligence, Not a Second Negotiation

The inspection period is for discovering material facts about the property — not for renegotiating price. Buyers who use minor inspection findings as an excuse to chip away at the agreed price erode goodwill and risk losing the deal. Focus on genuine health, safety, and major structural items.

Categorizing Inspection Items

CategoryExamplesStrategy
Safety/Code ItemsMissing smoke detectors, exposed wiring, broken handrailsRequest repair — these are legitimate asks

StructuralFoundation cracks, roof damageGet specialist report; serious issues may warrant price reduction or cancellation

Using the Request for Repair (C.A.R. Form RR)

The formal tool for post-inspection requests is the Request for Repair (C.A.R. Form RR). It allows buyers to ask the seller to:

  • Complete specific repairs before close
  • Provide a seller credit toward the buyer's closing costs
  • Reduce the purchase price
  • A combination of the above
RPA Reference: RPA ¶14C governs the investigation contingency. The RR form is submitted within the contingency period. Sellers can accept, reject, or counter the request. If no agreement is reached, buyers can cancel under the contingency and receive their EMD back.
✅ Negotiating a Credit vs. Repairs

A seller credit is often cleaner than repair completion. Sellers may use cheap contractors; buyers can't control quality. A credit of $5,000–$10,000 toward closing costs lets buyers hire their own contractor after closing. Present this as an option when requesting.

⚠️ Don't Overreach

Demanding that sellers fix every item on a 47-item inspection report signals bad faith. Sellers can reject the entire request and force you to either approve or cancel. Focus your request on 2–4 significant items and present it professionally. Reasonable requests get completed; unreasonable ones get deals cancelled.
