The Loan Approval Process — From Application to Funding

What happens inside the black box of loan underwriting — and how to keep transactions on track
~10 min readRPA ¶14L(1) / DRE Chapter 12 / B&P §101315 Quiz Questions
📋 Setting the Scene

It's Day 12 of the Okonkwos' escrow. Their lender emails: "Loan is in underwriting — we'll have an answer in 3-5 business days." Marcus asks: "What does that mean? What are they actually looking at?"

Knowing the underwriting process helps you manage client anxiety — and helps you know when to escalate if things get slow.

The Loan Processing Stage

After the loan application is submitted and the Loan Estimate is issued, the loan enters processing. A loan processor works on behalf of the lender to:

  • Collect required documents from the borrower (pay stubs, W-2s, tax returns, bank statements)
  • Order the appraisal
  • Order the title report
  • Verify employment (often called a VOE — Verification of Employment)
  • Package the complete file for submission to the underwriter

The Underwriting Stage

Underwriting is where the lender actually decides whether to approve the loan. The underwriter reviews the complete file against Fannie/Freddie guidelines (for conventional) or FHA/VA guidelines (for government loans). They analyze:

  • The 3 C's: Credit (credit score, history), Capacity (income, employment, DTI), Collateral (appraisal, property type)
  • All documentation for accuracy and completeness
  • The appraisal report
  • The title report
Underwriter DecisionWhat It Means
ApprovedLoan is approved as-is — rare without any conditions
Approved with Conditions (PTD)Loan is approved subject to satisfying specific conditions (most common outcome)
SuspendedMore information needed before a decision can be made
DeniedLoan is declined — borrower must address issues and reapply

Common Underwriting Conditions

An "approved with conditions" (also called PTD — Prior to Document) outcome is by far the most common result. Common conditions include:

  • Explanation letter for a gap in employment
  • Updated bank statements showing funds are still available
  • Verification that a large deposit is not a loan
  • HOA financial statement or insurance certificate
  • Additional year of tax returns
  • Letter explaining a prior credit issue
📌 Clear to Close (CTC)

"Clear to Close" means all conditions have been satisfied and the lender is ready to fund. CTC is the green light — loan documents go to escrow, buyer signs, lender wires funds. CTC is the moment you've been waiting for.

RPA Reference: The loan contingency deadline under RPA ¶14L(1) is typically 17 days after acceptance. If the lender hasn't removed conditions by then, you may need to request an extension in writing — or the seller can serve a Notice to Perform (NBP). Stay in daily contact with the lender during underwriting to catch delays early.

Large Deposits — A Common Underwriting Trap

Underwriters scrutinize large deposits in bank statements. If your clients' accounts show an unexplained large deposit (anything unusual that could be an undisclosed loan), the underwriter will ask for documentation. Coach your clients upfront:

  • Don't move money between accounts unnecessarily
  • Document any gifts with a gift letter from the donor
  • Don't take cash advances on credit cards — these show as liabilities
  • If they sold something (car, collectibles), keep the documentation
✅ Call Your Lender Weekly

During the underwriting period, call your lender contact every 2-3 days. Don't wait for them to call you. Ask: "What conditions are still outstanding? What does the borrower need to provide? Are we on track for the close date?" Proactive communication prevents surprises on Day 17.
