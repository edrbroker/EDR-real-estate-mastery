What Is Escrow?

Understanding the neutral third party that holds everything together in a California real estate transaction
~10 min readRPA ¶3B / Escrow Instructions5 Quiz Questions
📋 Setting the Scene

Marcus and Priya Okonkwo just got their offer accepted on 5555 Winners Lane, Los Angeles, CA 90210. They're excited — but then Priya asks you, "So… what exactly happens next? Who's actually holding our money?"

Great question. The answer is: escrow. And as their agent, it's your job to explain it clearly.

Escrow: The Neutral Referee

Escrow is a process where a neutral third party — called the escrow holder — holds money, documents, and instructions from both the buyer and seller until all conditions of the sale are met. Think of escrow like a referee at a championship game. They don't play for either team, but nothing counts unless they say so.

📌 Key Definition

The escrow holder cannot release the buyer's money to the seller until every condition in the purchase contract has been satisfied. That's what makes it safe for both sides.

Who Can Hold Escrow in California?

In California, escrow can be handled by several types of licensed entities:

  • Independent escrow companies — licensed by the Department of Financial Protection and Innovation (DFPI) under the California Escrow Law (Financial Code §17000)
  • Title companies — often handle both title insurance and escrow
  • Attorney escrows — less common in California residential transactions
  • DRE-licensed brokers — can perform escrow as part of a transaction they are already involved in (Financial Code §17006 exemption)
DRE Note: Under Financial Code §17403.4, all written escrow instructions must include the escrow company's license name and supervising agency. Always verify your escrow company is properly licensed.

What Does Escrow Actually Do?

The escrow officer is the central hub of your transaction. They:

  • Receive and hold the buyer's Earnest Money Deposit (EMD)
  • Collect and organize all required documents (grant deed, loan docs, title reports)
  • Coordinate with the buyer's lender to satisfy loan conditions
  • Order the preliminary title report and title insurance
  • Calculate and prorate property taxes, HOA dues, and other fees
  • Prepare the final settlement statement (closing disclosure)
  • Authorize recording of the deed with the county once all conditions are met
  • Disburse funds to the seller, pay off liens, and pay commissions
✓ Real Life Example

When Marcus and Priya's EMD of $37,500 is wired to escrow, it sits in the escrow trust account. It doesn't go to the seller. It doesn't go to you. It stays there — protected — until escrow closes or the deal cancels.

Opening Escrow

Escrow is typically opened within 1–3 business days of acceptance. Your office or the listing agent will send the fully executed purchase agreement to the escrow company. The escrow officer then sends both parties their escrow instructions to review and sign. Per RPA ¶3N(2), the buyer must sign and return escrow instructions within 5 days of delivery.

⚠️ Watch Out

Escrow instructions are a separate document from the RPA. They're prepared by the escrow company and must be signed by both buyer and seller. If your client doesn't sign them promptly, it can delay the entire transaction.
