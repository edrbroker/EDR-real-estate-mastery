Negotiating Timelines and Extensions

How to manage deadlines, request extensions professionally, and protect your clients from missed contingencies
~10 min readRPA ¶14 / C.A.R. Form CRTSA / ADDM5 Quiz Questions
📋 Setting the Scene

Escrow is day 14. The Okonkwos' lender calls — they need 5 more days to finish underwriting. The inspection contingency deadline is tomorrow. The sellers are not happy. What do you do?

Timeline management is where transactions either close smoothly or fall apart. Great agents prevent this. Good agents solve it quickly when it happens.

The Standard RPA Timeline

The RPA establishes default contingency periods in ¶14. All run from the acceptance date:

ContingencyRPA ParagraphDefault Period
Loan Contingency¶14L(1)17 days
Appraisal Contingency¶14L(2)17 days
Inspection Contingency¶14L(3)17 days
Insurance Contingency¶14L(4)17 days
Review of Seller Docs¶14L(5)17 days or 5 days after delivery
Close of Escrow¶3BAs agreed — typically 30 days
📌 Deadlines Are Real

Failing to remove a contingency by the deadline does NOT automatically cancel the contract — but it does allow the seller to serve a Notice to Buyer to Perform (NBP). The buyer then has 2 days to remove the contingency or face cancellation. This is where deals get tense.

Requesting an Extension

When a deadline cannot be met, the professional move is to request an extension in writing using the C.A.R. Form ADDM (Addendum). Both parties must sign. Key principles:

  • Ask early: Don't wait until the deadline passes to request an extension
  • Be specific: State exactly why you need the extension and how many days are needed
  • Be reasonable: Asking for 3–5 additional days is usually accepted; asking for 2 weeks raises red flags
  • Get it in writing: Verbal agreements to extend do not modify the contract
RPA Reference: Any modification to the contract — including timeline extensions — must be in writing and signed by both parties to be effective. A text message saying "no problem, take a few more days" has no legal force.

The CRTSA — Contingency Removal and Timeline Addendum

The C.A.R. Form CRTSA (Contingency Removal and Timeline Addendum) allows parties to formally extend or modify multiple contingency deadlines in a single document. Use this when several timelines need adjustment — for example, when the loan and inspection timelines both need to move together.

⚠️ Never Let Deadlines Slip Without Written Modification

If a contingency deadline passes without formal written removal or extension, the seller can serve an NBP and trigger a 2-day clock. If the buyer still doesn't respond, the seller can unilaterally cancel — and may have a claim to the EMD. Always get extensions in writing, signed by both parties, before the deadline.

✅ Proactive Timeline Management

The best agents set calendar reminders for every contingency deadline the moment escrow opens. They communicate with lenders and inspectors in the first 48 hours to confirm availability. They request extensions on Day 12–13 if needed — not on Day 17. Prevention is always cheaper than crisis management.
