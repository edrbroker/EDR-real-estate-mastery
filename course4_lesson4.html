Title Vesting — How Your Buyer Takes Ownership

Joint tenancy, community property, tenancy in common — and why it matters more than agents think
~10 min readRPA ¶13 / Civil Code §6825 Quiz Questions
📋 The Question

The escrow officer emails Marcus and Priya a form asking how they want to take title. They forward it to you and ask, "What should we put?" This is one of those moments where you need to know the options — and know the limits of your role.

Why Vesting Matters

How your buyers take title determines what happens to the property if one owner dies, gets divorced, or has a creditor issue. It affects estate planning, taxes, and legal rights. As an agent, you must present the options — but you cannot advise which to choose. Always recommend they consult an attorney or tax advisor.

The Main Vesting Options in California

Community Property

Available to married couples and registered domestic partners. Each spouse owns 50% and the property gets a full step-up in tax basis when one spouse dies (valuable tax benefit).

Community Property With Right of Survivorship

Same as community property but adds automatic transfer to the surviving spouse without probate. Combines the tax benefit of community property with the simplicity of survivorship.

Joint Tenancy

Available to any two or more people (doesn't have to be married). All owners have equal shares. When one owner dies, their share automatically passes to the surviving owners without probate.

Tenancy in Common

Each owner can hold unequal shares (e.g., 70%/30%). Each share can be sold or inherited separately. No right of survivorship — when an owner dies, their share goes to their estate, not the other owners.

⚠️ Stay in Your Lane

Never tell your client which vesting to choose. This is a legal and tax decision. Tell them the options exist, explain briefly what each means, and direct them to their attorney. If you give vesting advice and something goes wrong, you could face liability.
