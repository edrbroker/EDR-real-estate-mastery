The Seller Property Questionnaire (SPQ)

Going deeper than the TDS — additional seller disclosures that fill the gaps
~10 min readC.A.R. Form SPQ / RPA ¶14A5 Quiz Questions
📋 Setting the Scene

The Okonkwos have reviewed the TDS and NHD. Now their agent hands them another disclosure form: the Seller Property Questionnaire. Priya asks, "Didn't we already get the TDS? Why do we need this too?"

Great question. The TDS is the foundation — the SPQ goes deeper into specific issues the TDS doesn't fully address.

What Is the SPQ?

The Seller Property Questionnaire (C.A.R. Form SPQ) is a supplemental disclosure form that asks sellers more specific questions about the property's history, condition, and any known issues. While the TDS focuses on physical systems, the SPQ digs into history, litigation, neighbor disputes, and more nuanced property facts.

📌 SPQ vs. TDS

Think of the TDS as the property's health checkup, and the SPQ as the detailed medical history. They work together. In California, C.A.R. recommends sellers complete both. The SPQ is not technically required by statute the way the TDS is — but failing to disclose known facts covered by the SPQ can still create liability.

Key Topics the SPQ Covers

  • Water intrusion and drainage: Any history of leaks, flooding, or drainage problems
  • Pest and wood-destroying organisms: Prior infestations, treatment history
  • Permits and room additions: Were all improvements done with permits and final inspections?
  • Boundary disputes: Any disagreements with neighbors over lot lines or easements
  • Noise and nuisance: Airport flight paths, busy roads, nearby commercial activity
  • Deaths on property: Certain deaths must be disclosed (see Lesson 6)
  • Mold: Any known mold or conditions conducive to mold growth
  • Insurance claims: Prior claims on homeowner's policy
  • Litigation: Any current or threatened lawsuits involving the property
⚠️ Permit Pull Alert

One of the most common SPQ issues: unpermitted additions (extra rooms, garage conversions, ADUs). If the seller built something without permits, it affects the home's legal square footage, insurance, and sometimes lender approval. Always ask about permits for any addition or major renovation.

How to Use the SPQ as a Buyer's Agent

When you receive the SPQ for 5555 Winners Lane, read it line by line. Any "yes" answer should prompt a follow-up. Your job is to make sure your clients understand what they're buying.

SPQ "Yes" AnswerBuyer's Agent Follow-Up
Prior roof leakRequest repair documentation and inspection report
Unpermitted additionVerify with city, consider retroactive permit, price adjustment
Prior insurance claimGet claim details — may affect future insurability
Neighbor disputeRequest details; could affect title or peaceful enjoyment
Litigation threatenedConsult with your broker — may be a red flag
✅ Agent Tip — Sellers Too

When you represent a seller, coach them to answer every SPQ question honestly. "I don't know" is an acceptable answer if they genuinely don't know — but guessing wrong or deliberately omitting facts creates massive legal exposure.
