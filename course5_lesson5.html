Lead, Mold, and Environmental Disclosures

Federal and state requirements for disclosing hazardous materials in residential properties
~10 min readCFR Title 40 Part 745 / Health & Safety Code / RPA ¶10A5 Quiz Questions
📋 Setting the Scene

5555 Winners Lane was built in 1968. When the Okonkwos' agent runs a quick title search, they see it's a pre-1978 property. That triggers a specific federal disclosure requirement that every California agent must know cold.

Lead-Based Paint Disclosure (Federal Law)

Under Federal CFR Title 40, Part 745 (the Residential Lead-Based Paint Hazard Reduction Act), sellers of residential properties built before 1978 must:

  • Provide buyers with the EPA pamphlet "Protect Your Family From Lead in Your Home"
  • Disclose any known lead-based paint hazards on the property using the Lead-Based Paint Disclosure form
  • Give buyers a 10-day opportunity (can be waived) to conduct a lead-based paint inspection
📌 When Does This Apply?

Any residential property (1–4 units) built before January 1, 1978. This includes properties that have been renovated — if the structure itself predates 1978, the disclosure applies.

⚠️ Federal Penalty

Agents who fail to provide lead disclosure for pre-1978 properties can face federal fines up to $11,000 per violation. This is one of the few real estate disclosure violations with direct federal enforcement.

Mold Disclosure

California does not have a specific statute requiring mold disclosure in the same way lead is handled federally — but mold is a material fact that must be disclosed on the TDS and SPQ if known. The state has set guidelines for permissible mold levels under the California Department of Public Health.

Visible mold, known mold remediation history, and conditions conducive to mold (chronic moisture, poor ventilation) must all be disclosed. Buyers are advised to order a mold inspection during the inspection contingency period.

✅ Practical Tip

If you see discoloration, musty smells, or evidence of water intrusion during your AVID walkthrough — note it and recommend a mold inspection. This protects both your client and you.

Other Environmental Disclosures

HazardDisclosure Requirement
AsbestosDisclose if known (common in pre-1980 insulation, floor tiles, popcorn ceilings)
Underground storage tanksDisclose if known; may require DTSC remediation
RadonNo mandatory disclosure, but material if tested and elevated
Military ordnance nearbyRequired disclosure if within 1 mile of a military training area
Methamphetamine contaminationHealth & Safety Code §25400.28 — must disclose if unit was used as a meth lab
RPA Reference: RPA ¶10A addresses environmental hazards generally and references attached disclosures. For pre-1978 properties, the Lead Disclosure Addendum must accompany the RPA.
