How Home Loans Work — The Big Picture

What a mortgage actually is, who the players are, and what your role as an agent looks like in the financing process
~10 min readDRE RE-22 / B&P Code §101315 Quiz Questions
📋 Setting the Scene

Marcus and Priya Okonkwo are ready to buy 5555 Winners Lane at $1,250,000. Marcus earned his MBA and runs his own business. Priya works in tech. They're smart people — but they've never bought a home before.

They call you and ask: "Where do we even start with the loan?" This is Lesson 1 of every buyer relationship. And how you answer it sets the tone for the whole transaction.

What Is a Mortgage?

In simple terms, a mortgage is a loan used to buy real property, where the property itself serves as collateral. If the borrower stops making payments, the lender can take the property through a legal process called foreclosure.

In California, most home loans use a deed of trust (not technically a "mortgage" but they work similarly). The deed of trust involves three parties: the borrower (trustor), the lender (beneficiary), and a neutral trustee who holds legal title until the loan is paid off.

📌 Why This Matters to You

As an agent, you are not a lender and you do not give loan advice. But you need to understand the basics so you can guide your clients to the right resources, help them ask the right questions, and stay in your lane. The DRE requires that you refer clients to licensed lenders for all financing decisions — you recommend, you don't advise.

Your Role in the Financing Process

As a real estate agent, your job is to:

  • Get buyers pre-approved FIRST — before showing any homes. A pre-approval letter tells you what price range is realistic.
  • Explain the process — help buyers understand the general steps and timeline.
  • Connect buyers with lenders — you can recommend lenders you've worked with, but not pressure clients to use any specific one.
  • Stay in your lane — you are not licensed to advise on interest rates, loan types, or whether a specific loan is right for your client. That is the lender's job.
✅ The Pre-Approval Rule

Make it a hard rule: no home tours without a pre-approval letter. This protects your time, helps your clients shop realistically, and strengthens their offer position. In a competitive market, a pre-approval isn't optional — it's expected.

⚠️ Know Your Limits

Telling a buyer "this loan is a good deal for you" or "you should go with the FHA loan" crosses into mortgage loan originator territory under B&P Code §10131 and the SAFE Act. Provide information — not advice — and always refer to the lender for any financial recommendation.

The Basic Loan Timeline

Once your client has found a property and signed a purchase agreement, here's what happens on the lending side:

  1. Buyer submits full loan application to lender
  2. Lender orders appraisal, reviews income docs, runs credit
  3. Underwriter reviews the full file and issues conditions
  4. Buyer satisfies conditions (pay stubs, bank statements, etc.)
  5. Lender issues "clear to close" (CTC)
  6. Lender sends loan documents to escrow for signing
  7. Lender funds the loan — wire to escrow
  8. Escrow records the deed — buyer owns the home
