Protecting Buyers in the Financing Process

Fair lending laws, red flags, and your duty to guide clients through the most important financial decision of their lives
~10 min readECOA / Fair Housing Act / RESPA / B&P §101765 Quiz Questions
📋 Setting the Scene

It's closing week for the Okonkwos. Everything is in order — loan is funded, CD is clean. As you hand over the keys, Marcus says: "You know, that whole loan process was terrifying. We had no idea what was happening most of the time." This is your reminder of what your role really is: protecting buyers through the most complex financial transaction of their lives.

Federal Fair Lending Laws

Two federal laws protect borrowers from discrimination in the lending process:

Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA / Regulation B)

ECOA prohibits lenders from discriminating against credit applicants based on race, color, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age, or receipt of public assistance. Lenders must:

  • Notify applicants of credit decisions within 30 days
  • Provide adverse action notices explaining why credit was denied
  • Give applicants a copy of their appraisal upon request

Fair Housing Act

The Fair Housing Act prohibits discrimination in the sale, rental, AND financing of housing based on race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, and disability. Redlining — refusing to make loans in certain geographic areas based on racial composition — is a federal violation.

DRE Reference: The DRE's RE-22 publication reminds buyers that they have the right to know why credit is denied and to report suspected lending discrimination to HUD or the CFPB. As an agent, if you suspect a lender is treating your client differently because of a protected characteristic, report it to your broker immediately.

RESPA — The Anti-Kickback Law

The Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act (RESPA) governs mortgage transactions. Key RESPA rules that directly affect real estate agents:

  • Section 8 — No kickbacks: It is illegal for any settlement service provider (including real estate agents) to give or receive anything of value in exchange for referring clients to a specific lender, title company, or escrow
  • Affiliated Business Arrangements (ABAs): If you refer clients to a business you are affiliated with (e.g., your broker owns the title company), you must disclose that relationship in writing
  • No requirement to use referred providers: Buyers cannot be required to use any specific service provider
⚠️ Kickbacks Are a Federal Crime

Accepting anything of value — cash, gifts, marketing, referral fees — from a lender or settlement service in exchange for referrals violates RESPA Section 8. This includes co-marketing arrangements that aren't structured correctly. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) actively enforces this. If you're unsure whether an arrangement is compliant, ask your broker before you agree to anything.

Red Flags Your Buyers Should Know About

The DRE's consumer publications warn buyers about predatory lending practices. Coach your clients to watch for:

Red FlagWhat It Means
Bait-and-switch on interest rateRate changes at signing from what was quoted — compare LE to CD
Pressure to waive the right to review CDBuyers always have 3 business days — no legitimate lender should pressure a waiver
Excessive points or fees not disclosed upfrontAll material costs must be on the Loan Estimate
"No cost" loans with very high ratesCosts are built into the rate — compare APR carefully
Asking for advance fees before closingBrokers must have DRE approval to collect advance fees — per RE-35A
✅ Your Most Important Job

You are not a lender. You do not give financial advice. But you are your clients' guide through the most important financial transaction of their lives. That means helping them ask the right questions, connecting them with qualified professionals, catching errors before they close, and never pushing them toward a lender or product that benefits you at their expense.
